# For the doctors: what deserves a supervisor's attention (draft rubric, 2026-11-26)

**Purpose:** to help us design the rubric. This is not to rate the app. The app's own answers to
the cases are in a separate file, opened only after you have given yours. These cases are for
designing the rubric and are kept apart from the cases we will score with it.

**The setting:**
- A supervisor sees weekly figures for their whole team.
- Each figure compares every person with their own usual weeks, and the team figure is the
  median.
- No figure is shown from fewer than 5 people.
- No one person is ever named.
- The app may suggest one team-wide change (for example, no meetings after 5 pm). It waits for
  the supervisor's approval.

## Three questions per case

1. **Attention.** Which finding, if any, deserves the supervisor's attention this week?
   - 0: none;
   - 1: worth noting;
   - 2: worth acting on.

   Why?
2. **Wording.** What may honestly be said from these readings?
   - Mark each statement *supported*, *too strong* or *too weak*.
   - Is "no clear change" right when, say, 4 of 5 people moved one way?
   - Should a figure from exactly 5 people be shown with any caution?
3. **Action.** Would a team-wide change be reasonable? Rate it:
   - 0: inappropriate;
   - 1: acceptable;
   - 2: well suited.

   Is there a better one, or none at all? (The app can suggest: an evening meeting boundary, a
   meeting-free day, breaks between meetings, a team step goal.)

Also tell us:
- anything a supervisor should **never** be told or asked to do from data like this;
- when the right answer is to say nothing, or that there is too little to tell.

R cases are real public data:
- LifeSnaps, Yfantidou et al. 2022, CC BY 4.0;
- teams formed by us, not real colleagues.

S cases are made up, and labelled as such.
